## Abstract

The Kerr spin length *a* = *J*/(*Mc*), introduced in 1963 as a parameter of rotating spacetimes, is defined for any system carrying angular momentum — collapsed or not. Here we examine what follows when this length is taken seriously for ordinary rotating matter across all scales.

For any body in orbital motion, *J* = *MvR*, so

> *a* = *vR*/*c*

The mass cancels exactly. We call this the **Mass Cancellation Theorem**: the angular-duality wavelength λ_ad ≡ *vR*/*c* depends only on geometry and motion, not on the test mass. Every participant in the same rotation — from a small rotor to a planet — shares this rotational length scale.

In Kerr's horizon formula, any non-collapsed rotating body satisfies *a* ≫ *M*_g ≡ *GM*/*c*², placing it in the regime where the horizon roots become complex. The magnitude of the imaginary part equals *a*, providing a real geometric scale even when no classical horizon exists.

De Broglie's matter wavelength λ_dB = *h*/(*Mv*) shares the same dimensional structure as λ_ad but is a distinct quantity, equal only on the exceptional borderline *Jv* = *hc*. New calculations in this version show that for tungsten nanoparticles (35–200 nm radius) and graphene nanodisks this borderline is reached at laboratory velocities of approximately 0.06–36 m/s.

The present work derives and interprets λ_ad on its own geometric terms, while keeping the deep dimensional kinship with de Broglie clearly visible.

## 3. Mass Cancellation Theorem

**Theorem.** For ordinary orbital motion (with *J* = *MvR*) the angular-duality wavelength satisfies

> λ_ad = *vR*/*c*

and is independent of the inertial mass. For rigid-body rotation, the same length appears multiplied by a dimensionless shape factor κ (with κ = 1 for a thin ring, κ = 1/2 for a uniform disk, κ = 2/5 for a uniform sphere); the mass still cancels.

**Proof (orbital).** For a body of mass *M* in a circular orbit of radius *R* at tangential velocity *v*, the angular momentum is *J* = *MvR*. Substitution gives

> λ_ad = *MvR*/(*Mc*) = *vR*/*c*

The mass cancels exactly.

**Proof (rigid spin).** For a body with angular speed ω and moment of inertia *I* = κ*MR*²,

> λ_ad = *I*ω/(*Mc*) = κ*R*²ω/*c* = κ *Rv*/*c*

Again the mass cancels; the only difference from the orbital case is the shape factor κ.

### 3.1 Electron and Proton (Intrinsic Spin)

For spin-½ particles, the quantum-mechanical magnitude of angular momentum is

> |*J*| = ℏ√(*s*(*s*+1)) = ℏ√3/2

As an order-of-magnitude lower bound one may use the commonly cited approximation *J* ≈ ℏ/2 (the z-projection):

- λ_ad^(e) ≈ ℏ/2 / (*m*_e *c*) ≈ 2×10⁻¹³ m (full magnitude: 3×10⁻¹³ m)
- λ_ad^(p) ≈ ℏ/2 / (*m*_p *c*) ≈ 1×10⁻¹⁶ m (full magnitude: 2×10⁻¹⁶ m)

These are illustrative only; a rigorous quantum treatment of the Kerr scale in the particle regime lies outside the present scope and constitutes an open problem in quantum-gravity phenomenology.

### 3.2 Precedent in the Literature for the Elementary-Particle Case

The structural coincidence *a* ≈ λ_C for spinning elementary particles has peer-reviewed precedent. Carter (1968) showed that the Kerr–Newman geometry carrying the electron's mass, charge, and spin reproduces its measured gyromagnetic ratio *g* = 2. Burinskii's Dirac–Kerr–Newman electron program places the electron's ring structure at the Compton scale and develops the same correspondence in detail. The A-series derives the universality of the rotational Kerr scale from the macroscopic regime first; the electron case appears as a consistency check with established work, not as a primary claim about particle structure.

### 3.3 Laboratory Rotor

For the 1 g DNA-origami hydrogel ring studied in companion paper A2 (*R* = 8 mm, *v* = 0.45 m/s), treated as a thin ring (κ = 1):

> λ_ad = *Rv*/*c* = (8×10⁻³ × 0.45) / (3×10⁸) ≈ 1.2×10⁻¹¹ m

A2 explores a different resonance condition (λ_dB = *R*_s) and should be read independently.

### 3.4 Earth and Galaxy

For Earth's orbital motion about the Sun:

> λ_ad^(⊙) = *v*_orb *R*_orb / *c* ≈ 1.5×10⁷ m

For galactic rotation (Milky Way, representative values *v*_gal ~ 220 km/s, galactic radii in the range 8–15 kpc):

> λ_ad^(Gal) = *v*_gal *R*_gal / *c* ~ (1.8–3.4)×10¹⁷ m ≈ 20–36 light-years

a macroscopic length attached purely to rotation. The precise value depends on which portion of the galactic disk is sampled; the range is noted without claiming a unique value.

*Notes on scope.* The quantum/particle estimations are illustrative, left for possible refinement in a dedicated future article or by a specialized team.

*Interpretation.* The theorem implies that, for any given rotation, the angular-duality wavelength λ_ad is the same for all participants (up to the shape factor κ for rigid-body cases).

Consider a pebble resting on a shore — visibly still, its crystalline structure a silent record of eons of geological rotation. The sea wave rolling over it, the seagull turning above, the mountain at the horizon, the asteroid crossing overhead, the spacecraft in orbit, the planet beneath: each participates in different rotations at different scales, yet each shares the same angular-duality wavelength λ_ad = κ*vR*/*c* for whatever rotation it belongs to, with contributions from orbital, planetary, and galactic motion overlaid without contradiction.

## 5. A Borderline Relation: When the Two Scales Coincide

λ_dB and λ_ad are not equal in general. Forcing equality at a special point,

> λ_dB = λ_ad → *Jv* = *hc*

one obtains a borderline in (*J*, *v*) parameter space rather than a universal identity.

This exceptional condition — the matching between the Kerr rotational scale and the de Broglie wavelength — was explored across the A-series and provides a concrete point where the geometric rotational scale can intersect with quantum wave behavior. Using λ_ad = κ*Rv*/*c* and λ_dB = *h*/(*Mv*), the borderline velocity is

> *v** = √(*hc* / (κ *mR*))

For the nanoparticle table below, we use the disk-approximation shape factor κ = 1/2 appropriate for graphene nanodisks and a reasonable approximation for tungsten nanospheres of order unity. This gives

> **v* = √(2*hc* / (*mR*))** (κ = 1/2, disk approximation)

The simpler orbital convention (κ = 1) would give *v** = √(*hc*/(*mR*)), smaller by a factor of √2.

## 7. Imaginary Kerr Roots as a Physical Signal

The Kerr horizon radii are

> *r*± = *GM*/*c*² ± √((*GM*/*c*²)² − (*J*/(*Mc*))²)

Defining *M*_g ≡ *GM*/*c*² and *a* ≡ *J*/(*Mc*), this is *r*± = *M*_g ± √(*M*_g² − *a*²).

**Ordinary matter.** For any non-collapsed rotating body, *a* ≫ *M*_g, so

> *r*± = *M*_g ± *i*√(*a*² − *M*_g²) ≈ *M*_g ± *ia*

This is not an approximation of general relativity; it is its direct algebraic outcome for ordinary rotating matter.

**Physical content.** The geometry does not disappear when the roots become complex. The Kerr solution retains a real, measurable scale:

> |Im *r*±| ≈ *a* = λ_ad

**Rotation generates a real curvature scale even when no classical horizon exists.**

The imaginary value signals a change of regime — curvature is no longer a spacetime surface but an extended or evanescent structure — not the absence of physics. The masses involved remain positive and ordinary. What changes is not the mass but its geometric regime: rotation dominates gravitational self-compression.

This is genuinely not covered by current textbooks; it is an open domain, not a closed one. Dirac's negative-energy solutions appeared unphysical until they predicted the positron. The imaginary Kerr horizon, likewise, may encode real physics waiting to be read properly. To declare a mathematical result of general relativity "non-physical" because it surpasses current interpretive frameworks is not a scientific position; it is a statement about the limits of the framework, not the limits of nature. Wheeler, Hawking, Penrose, and Thorne all insisted that the full solution space of general relativity must be kept open. The present result is simply that solution, applied without truncation to ordinary rotating matter. A field of genuine research awaits in this unexplored regime.

**Why this is not a "naked singularity."** That term applies to collapsed point objects where the vacuum Kerr solution is extended to *r* = 0. For extended bodies, interior matter replaces the vacuum solution long before any singularity is reached. The complex-root scale from the exterior geometry remains meaningful and unavoidable.

**Connection to spacetime foam.** Wheeler introduced "spacetime foam" to describe regimes where classical geometric notions break down. Hawking and Penrose showed that rotation and curvature, when dominant, push geometry beyond classical surfaces. The imaginary Kerr roots for ordinary rotators point to the same transition: a vacuum-supported structure whose scale is fixed by *J*/(*Mc*), arising without new fields or modifications to Einstein's equations.

Because λ_ad = κ*vR*/*c* is mass-independent, it invites comparison with vacuum stress scales, which are also mass-independent. Idealized Casimir formulas:

> *P*_Cas(*d*) = −π²ℏ*c* / (240 *d*⁴)

> *E*/*A*(*d*) = −π²ℏ*c* / (720 *d*³)

At Planck-scale separations (*d* ~ 10⁻³⁵ m), naive Casimir scaling yields energy densities many orders of magnitude above *mc*² for the associated vacuum granule, signalling the breakdown of effective field descriptions. Vacuum stresses at these scales are parametrically stronger than mass energy and capable, in principle, of coupling to rotation-defined scales. At femtometer separations, the force scale is comparable to known hadronic stresses. These estimates are documented to record the order-of-magnitude context; a full treatment requires quantum field theory development beyond the present scope.

## 10. Conclusion

The Kerr spin length *a* = *J*/(*Mc*) reduces, for ordinary orbital and rigid-body rotation, to λ_ad = κ*vR*/*c* — a scale independent of test mass, with shape factor κ depending on the body's moment of inertia (κ = 1 for orbital and ring, 1/2 for uniform disk, 2/5 for uniform sphere). In Kerr's horizon formula, ordinary rotators generically enter the complex-root regime; |Im *r*±| = *a* = λ_ad. Rotation therefore defines a universal geometric length at every scale — from electron spin to galactic motion — without new physics.

De Broglie's matter wavelength and the angular-duality wavelength share dimensional structure and an exceptional borderline of equality (*Jv* = *hc*), but are distinct quantities with distinct physical origins. New calculations confirm that for tungsten nanoparticles of 35–200 nm radius, the borderline is reached at velocities of 0.06–1.8 m/s, accessible with existing nanomechanical tools.

The relationship between de Broglie wavelength and Kerr rotational scales explored across the A-series forms a coherent geometric program, from the de Broglie–Schwarzschild matching of the early papers through the vacuum granule spectrum of A9. Equality holds only on the exceptional borderline discussed above, not as a universal identity. Kepler imagined the universe as a harmony of motions contributing to a greater order. What emerges here is not a single privileged scale but a layered hierarchy of rotational structures: atomic, planetary, stellar, and galactic, all coexisting without contradiction. Each system samples the rotational geometry written by larger motions while imprinting its own.
