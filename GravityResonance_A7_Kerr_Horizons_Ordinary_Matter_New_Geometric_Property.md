## 1. The Universal Inequality

For any system of rest mass *M* and angular momentum *J*, the Kerr outer horizon is

> *r*₊ = *GM*/*c*² + √((*GM*/*c*²)² − (*J*/(*Mc*))²)

The spin length *a* = *J*/(*Mc*) exceeds the gravitational length *R*_g = *GM*/*c*² by many orders of magnitude in every realistic non-collapsed system. Thus

> *r*₊ ≈ *i J*/(*Mc*) (imaginary)

An imaginary horizon radius corresponds to a negative contribution in the effective stress-energy tensor. This is a property of the Kerr geometry as applied to ordinary rotating matter — the same mass that has always existed, positive and unchanged, now described by an additional geometric expression that the equations make available.

The mathematical existence of complex horizon roots when *a* ≫ *GM*/*c*² has been standard in the literature since Kerr's original paper (1963) and is treated by Chandrasekhar, Wald, and Teukolsky. The present work applies this regime to ordinary, non-collapsed rotating matter — a domain usually discussed only for astrophysical black holes.

The result is structurally analogous to Dirac's negative-energy solutions: the sign that initially appeared unphysical encoded the deepest physics, predicting the positron. Here, the imaginary Kerr horizon encodes a real geometric scale in a regime that remains largely unexplored for ordinary matter.

**What this means physically.** Every rotating body satisfies *a* ≫ *R*_g. Every such body therefore carries an imaginary Kerr horizon as a geometric characteristic. This is not a claim that mass disappears or reverses sign in any classical sense. The mass remains positive and ordinary; what we have identified is an additional geometric property of rotating matter. A stone did not change when we decoded its crystalline structure; the crystal was there all along, waiting to be read. Where this newly identified trait leads in terms of physics and applications is an open question the A-series opens for future investigation.

## 2. Path to the Discovery

The route to the result did not begin with Kerr geometry, but with an empirical puzzle.

### 2.1 Universal 1/*f* Noise and the 1–5 Hz Excess

Across mechanical sensors, gravimeters, seismographs, interferometers, and superconducting circuits, a broad, universal 1–5 Hz excess appears. It is too low for lattice modes, too high for geophysical tides, and too stable across different platforms and continents to be instrumental. This suggests an inertial origin at *f* ~ 1 Hz.

### 2.2 Reverse de Broglie Equation Gives a Mass Scale

The observed frequency *f* ~ 1 Hz is interpreted as the de Broglie frequency of the electrons (see A9). Louis de Broglie originally applied his relation to the center-of-mass motion of any system, not only to coherent quantum beams. Modern Sagnac ring-laser gyroscopes confirm this empirically for macroscopic rotating bodies.

Starting from the de Broglie relation

> λ_dB = *h*/(*Mv*)

the frequency seen by a detector is *f* = *v*/λ_dB. Substituting gives:

> *f* = *Mv*²/*h*  →  *M* = *hf*/*v*²

Using realistic drift or diffusion velocities of electrons, *v* ~ 10⁵–10⁶ m/s, with *f* ~ 1 Hz:

> *M* ~ 10⁻⁴⁴ kg

This value independently matches vacuum-condensate and loop-quantum-gravity grain mass estimates. Companion paper A9 reports a granule mass range of 10⁻⁵⁰–10⁻⁴³ kg with Compton wavelengths spanning 22 m to 22+ km.

For a single granule of mass *M* ≈ 10⁻⁴⁴ kg with angular momentum |*J*| ~ ℏ (for spin-½, the exact quantum mechanical value is |**J**| = (√3/2)ℏ ≈ 0.866ℏ; we use |*J*| ~ ℏ as an order-of-magnitude estimate), the imaginary Kerr horizon scale is:

> |*r*₊| ≈ ℏ/(*Mc*) ≈ 35 m

The Compton wavelength of the same granule is:

> λ_C = *h*/(*Mc*) ≈ 220 m

The two differ by exactly 2π = *h*/ℏ. This overlap between a purely quantum length (λ_C) and a geometric Kerr length (|*r*₊|) is not assumed — it emerges from the formulas. Both fall within the arm lengths of LIGO (4 km), Virgo (3 km), and the Einstein Telescope (10 km).
